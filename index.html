The problem

Scanning is easy. Knowing what to fix first is hard.

Point any scanner at a codebase and you get hundreds of findings. Most of them are noise, a few of them will ruin your week, and nothing in the output tells you which is which. That triage is the work — and it's the part that gets skipped.

Evidence, not alerts

Every finding arrives with the file, the line, and the commit it came from. You can confirm it in your own repository before you touch anything.

Written to be read

Scanner output is written for scanners. The report is written for the person who has to decide what happens on Monday morning.

Honest about limits

A static scan catches what static scans catch. Where the tool can't see, the report says so instead of implying clean.

Your code, briefly and only

The repository is cloned into a temporary workspace for the scan and deleted straight after — on every plan, paid ones included. Nothing is retained, nothing is reused.

Open scanners, checkable results

Detection runs on Gitleaks and Trivy. You can run the same tools yourself and get the same findings — the value added is the triage, not a black box.

Is my code stored anywhere?

No. It's cloned to a temporary workspace, scanned, and the clone is deleted immediately afterwards. That rule doesn't change on paid plans.

Does this work on private repositories?

Not on the free tier, which covers public repositories only. Private repository access is part of Pro and Business.

How long does a scan take?

Usually under two minutes. Repositories approaching the 300MB limit take longer, and deep commit histories add time on the secrets pass.

What counts as a vulnerability?

Publicly disclosed issues in your dependencies, tracked by CVE identifier, plus known misconfiguration patterns in infrastructure files. It's a match against established databases, not a guess.

Is a scan the same as a security audit?

No, and it's worth being blunt about it. A static scan finds what static scans find. It won't catch business logic flaws, broken access control, or anything that only shows up against a running system. That's what the assessments are for.

Which languages and frameworks are supported?

Dependency and infrastructure scanning covers what Trivy supports out of the box — npm and Node, Python, Java, Go, Ruby, plus Docker, Kubernetes and Terraform files. Secrets detection works across any file type.

Can I cancel whenever I want?

Yes. Pro and Business bill monthly with no contract, and you cancel from your own Stripe billing portal without asking anyone.
